# Draw a big book of the Bible around the place read

**What it is.** A book over 400 verses is not drawn whole. `components/bible/chapterWindow.ts` decides which chapters are drawn (whole chapters, about 60 verses on each side of the chapter at the reading line, at most 6 a side). `components/bible/useChapterWindow.ts` holds that span, the heights of the boxes, and the text kept still. `BookRibbon` (given `boxes`) draws every other chapter as `<section data-chapter data-box>` with its heading and a fixed height. The reader (`BibleBookReader`) and the verse window over a meeting (`VerseSheet`) use it.

**Why.** Every change that touches the whole text costs WebKit about 25 µs per element in the page, whether hidden or not. A new text size (a CSS variable on `<html>`) or an iPad turn in the Psalms (20,536 elements) took 682 ms and 835 ms. With the chapters around the place only (about 1,900 elements) they take 98 ms and 109 ms; with one chapter, about 50 ms (that is the rest of the screen). `content-visibility: auto` saved Chrome the same, but in WebKit 682 → 583 ms only: WebKit skips layout, not style, of skipped content. Measured on 2026-10-16, production build, Playwright WebKit and Chrome.

**Rules for code that touches the verses:**
- **A verse may not be in the page.** Before reaching it, go through the window: `show({ chapter }, then)`. The window draws the chapter, and `then` runs once it is in the page. In the reader that is `jumpTo(place, then)`; never `querySelector` a verse far from the place and act on `null`.
- **Rows read once go stale.** Whatever keeps a list of `[data-verse]` (the place read on scroll, the minimap, the marks' word index) reads it again when the drawn span changes (`boxes.span` → `drawn` prop, or `drawnKey` for `useMarkHighlights`).
- **The reading line can stand in a box.** `chapterAtLine(article, line)` says which chapter, and whether it is a box. The place is then that chapter.
- **The window follows the reading line at the moment of the frame** (`follow()` with no argument). Following a place noted earlier moved the window back after a jump.
- **The text the person sees must not move.** A chapter taken away leaves a box of its measured height, so nothing moves. A box of a guessed height drawn above the seen text moves it by the guess's error. `apply` keeps the first drawn verse on the screen still: it moves the scroll by the shift before paint, inside `flushSync`. While the scroll is moving, guessed boxes above the seen text wait until it rests (160 ms), because moving the scroll during an iPad glide may stop it. Safari had no scroll anchoring before 27; `overflow-anchor` is not relied on.
- **Only the window's own changes are kept still** (inside `apply`, before and after one `flushSync`). A page-wide hold of the seen text — an emulated scroll anchoring through a ResizeObserver — was built and removed: Codex's second attack found eight ways it broke. Among them: a pending frame replaced the anchor, a fractional sliver became the anchor, the browser's own clamping was taken for a user scroll, it broke smooth scrolls and pinned modals, and it missed content above the article on a phone. Do not bring it back without answering those.
- **The verse a jump came to stays at the top until the person scrolls** (`BibleBookReader`, ResizeObserver on the reader and the text, while `broughtRef` holds). Things that arrive after the jump grow the text above it: the links' chips, a late font, the book's line wrapping. Without this, a link opened on a phone landed 400–10,000 px above its verse, in the old ribbon too. `note()` clears `broughtRef` on any scroll, so the person's scroll wins — except the window's own: when `apply` moved the page to keep the line (`onDrawn`) while the verse holds, the reader jumps to the verse again (`holdBroughtRef`). The window's correction can miss by a few verses, and taken for a scroll it let the place go: a link to Synodal Ps 22:1 read in the King James stood at 23:1, then at 22:27, about one run in two (BUG-20261016-bible-deeplink-lands-early-verse, traced live 2026-10-17 by wrapping `scrollTo`/`scrollIntoView` in the page). `tools/webkit-lab/translations-same-place.js` checks it.
- **An outside page notice must also reach the held jump.** On the window surface, the existing held-jump observer watches `document.documentElement` and `document.body` in addition to the reader/text. A notice above the article can move the landing without resizing either article element; a fixed body can change while the document root keeps its height. This observation exists only while `broughtRef` holds, not as an always-on reading anchor. Actual later scrolling still releases it.
- **A pinned page has a logical reading offset even when `window.scrollY` is zero.** The existing `useScrollLock` owner exposes `pinnedPageScrollTop()`; the window branch of `readingSurface` reads it and moves that same owner through `scrollPinnedPageBy`. Unlock restores the corrected offset. Do not create another page-offset state or interpret native zero during pinning as deliberate reading. Pane surfaces retain their own `scrollTop`. The actual local notice/blocked-IndexedDB reproduction, pinned/unpinned checks and native wheel release are retained in Unit176; physical-device behavior remains a separate check.
- **Centered passages in sheets use `usePassageRest`**, shared by `VerseSheet` and `CrossReferenceSheet`. It observes both the pane and the text, owns native anchoring through `readingSurface.anchoring(false)` while held, and recomputes the center with `restingScrollTop`. A layout clamp is not a person's scroll: compare geometry too, and settle it before an input's default movement. Actual later movement releases the hold and restores native anchoring; the return button rearms it. Pass chapter-window corrections through `drawn(moved)` and gate late font callbacks on `isResting()`. Do not copy the old `scrollTop === restedAt` ownership rule or disable anchoring alone: in a real late-link probe that CSS-only control worsened a16px center error to455px because the app never recentered. Smooth-return interruption is limited to scrolling input; copying text must not retire the landing. Geometry test fixtures must deliver their initial observer measurement before modeling later deliberate reading.
- **A new width or text size** clears the measured heights and guesses the boxes again from the chapters drawn, then `onReflow` runs. The reader jumps back to its place; the verse window puts back the passage, or the verse at its top. The reader is the only owner of that: the old `useKeepPlaceOnTextScale` jumped a second time.
- **Read a scroll position only from the calibrated layout** (`canReadPosition()`). The chapter window binds reading evidence to its calibrated text and width; a native scroll from a different width or an unfinished chapter landing cannot replace it. The same width check runs before wheel, touch, pressed-pointer and keyboard input, settling reflow before the browser performs its default scroll; its later observer callback sees that width already handled and cannot undo the newer input. After box calibration and restoration settle, `onDrawn` lets readers record the resulting place again. A passage window keeps its verse key and its offset inside the pane together: aligning every restored verse to8px also moves a verse that had stood15px below the edge.
- **A jump waiting for its chapter wins over a reflow.** A new size arriving in the same moment must not replace it (`pendingRef ??=`).
- **Something else changed chapter heights** (the links' chips, the signs of marks): `doubt()`. The heights measured before are kept for their boxes, so nothing moves, but they no longer count as known for drawing above the text seen while scrolling.
- **A light kept by elements is lost when its chapter leaves.** The arrival light is kept by verse keys and painted again on the rows drawn.

**What it costs, knowingly.** Find in page (Cmd+F) and select-all find only the chapters drawn. A passage's light that is fading when its chapter leaves and comes back returns already faded.

**How to measure.** Use a production build in an isolated copy (`measure-typing-lag.md`). Two probes:
- `tools/webkit-lab/big-book-cost.js`: open time, text size cost and turn cost per book.
- `tools/webkit-lab/big-book-scroll.js`: wheel down and up past the drawn chapters, counting how often a row on the screen moved by anything other than the scroll, and how far the text moved at rest.

Count only rows that are on the screen before and after a step. A row past the edge moving is invisible, and an early probe counted it as a jump. Set the text size through the "A+" button, not by writing the variable directly: the place is restored only through the provider.

For a controlled native wheel acceptance, compare a still-visible row's viewport movement with the requested wheel delta, sampling its rectangle and scroll together. Keep raw scroll and document-height changes as diagnostics: virtual chapter/box replacement can change both by the same amount while preserving the visible row. `delta(rectTop) + delta(scroll)` measures document-coordinate change; it is not by itself a visible jump. Retain an idle-drift check, and avoid clamping/accelerated input when using the requested pixel delta. Phase50 atomic Chromium samples show ±46px document changes with exactly160px visible movement and0px idle drift.
