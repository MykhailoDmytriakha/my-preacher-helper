# Print, search or parse a Scripture reference

Print a reference through `formatScriptureReference(ref, { style, locale })` in `frontend/app/utils/scriptureReference.ts`; parse typed text with `parseReferenceText(raw)` in `frontend/app/utils/scriptureReferenceParser.ts`; book names and abbreviations are `frontend/app/utils/bibleBooks.ts`. Never build `book chapter:verse` by hand, and never renumber one by hand: `frontend/app/utils/scriptureReferenceNumbering.ts` does it for every Bible.

## How

- One reading of the shape, three faces. `short`: the book abbreviated in the interface language and glued to the chapter — chips, compact labels. `long`: the full localized book name — cards, previews. `canonical`: the stored English name and stored numbering ("Luke 5:17-26") — AI prompts and machine comparison only, never shown as the interface's words.
- WHICH BIBLE A REFERENCE IS WRITTEN AND SHOWN IN (the owner, 2026-10-17). Written: where the person writes it — on the Bible screen, the translation open there; anywhere else (a note's quick input, the picker, a meeting, the AI from a note), the Bible of the interface's language (`referenceBibleFor`). Shown: the screen's own Bible — notes, sermons, groups and councils by the interface's language, the Bible screen by the translation open there (each device keeps its own). The verse is the same everywhere: the reference carries the Bible it was written in, and every screen maps it. No writer on the Bible screen exists yet; the first (the reading's own notes, item 2.2) takes the open translation.
- A reference is kept as it was written, with the Bible it was written in: `{ bible: 'rst', book: 'Psalms', chapter: 50, fromVerse: 12 }`. The parser reads numbers as written and knows no Bible; whoever stores the result adds `bible: referenceBibleFor(locale)` (quick input, the picker, the dictation normalizer) — the AI analysis gets the Bible of the note's language on the server (`studyNote.structured.ts`).
- `short` and `long` show it in the Bible of the interface's language, `canonical` in the King James ("Psalms 51:10"), through `passagesIn(ref, bible)` in `scriptureReferenceNumbering.ts`: the exact verses the reference covers there, as passages. A passage may cross a chapter seam — Synodal "Пс 9:20-25" prints "Ps 9:19-10:4" (the owner, 2026-10-17: "9:19–10:4 is fine") — and a gap parts it into pieces: King James Romans 16:24-27 prints "Рим.14:24-26, 16:24". A whole chapter stays whole where it is one (Пс 22 → Ps 23, Пс 9 → Ps 9-10). A passage is printed and compared, never stored. `short` without a locale prints the numbers as stored, with the Russian abbreviation table.
- Compare two references with `sameScriptureReference`, never field by field: the same verses in the King James, a whole chapter being its first to last verse ("Пс 9" and "Пс 9:1-39" are one place). The verses a reference covers are `versesHeldIn(ref, bible)`, every one of them (the verse window lights them).
- For overlapping uses, `scriptureReferencesOverlap(a, b)` compares the verses in the common numbering, including whole chapters and references in different Bibles. Both inputs name their Bible.
- `referenceIn(ref, bible)` is the stored shape in another Bible, for a form that edits a reference (the picker): the shape has no verse range across a chapter seam, so there "Пс 9:20-25" opens as Ps 9-10 and a gap keeps the chapter holding most verses. The picker gives the reference back as it was written when the person changed nothing — only their own choices count, not the form's own straightening of its fields on open (BUG-20261017-reference-across-chapters-in-other-bible: saving what was shown widened 6 verses to 38).
- References stored before 2026-10-14 carry no `bible`: their Psalm chapter was moved by one, the verse left as typed ("Пс 50:12" kept as Psalms 51:12). Every read of a note names them once — `withNoteReferenceBibles(note, author)` — in the language the author's interface speaks (the owner, 2026-10-17: "a Ukrainian interface — the Ukrainian Bible"; the old picker moved a Psalm by it, and it names the Bible a person uses). In the app a note is read by its author alone, so the screen's language is theirs: `engineNote` (`hooks/useStudyNotesEngine.ts`; its baseline guard compares the stored references themselves, never a reading of them — a reading changes with the language and hides changes, old Psalm "9" and "10" both reading as Synodal 9 — against the stored list the editor's list was read from, `readFrom`), `useSermonNoteLinks`, the recovered draft. The server gets it from the author's request — `getAuthenticatedRequestHeaders` adds `x-app-language` from the browser's `lang` cookie (`utils/appLocale.ts`), and every route that reads a note passes `requestLanguage(request)` to `studiesRepository.getNote(id, author)` — and a public link keeps it from when it was made (`authorLanguage` on the link; the preview card speaks it too). Where it is not known (a link made before, a request without the cookie), the language of the note's words, English for a note with none. A note whose old references the AI took from Russian words under an English interface reads one Psalm off: the AI moved them by the words, the picker by the interface. The AI's own new references (`studyNote.structured.ts`) are numbers written in the text and take the text's language. An old ru/uk "Пс 147" was stored as 148 and stays 148.
- A verse wins over a leftover chapter range (old data carries both); `5:17-17` prints as one verse.
- Several references: `formatScriptureReferences(refs, { ...face, limit, separator })` returns `undefined` when there is nothing, so callers write `?? fallback`.
- Open a reference in the Bible through `frontend/app/utils/bibleLink.ts`, never by spelling `/bible?…`: `bibleHref(ref, { locale, from })` for a stored reference (one without `bible` is read through `withStoredBible`), `bibleHrefForText(raw, …)` for a reference kept as typed text (service order steps; a long dash between verses is read as a hyphen), `leadingReference(text, …)` for the passage a sermon's verse opens with (only its first words, a chapter required). The address carries `at` (the verse), `to` (the last verse lit on arrival), `tr` (the Bible the numbers are in) and `from` (the way back, an app path only); the Bible page reads `to` and `from` with the same module.
- A book as people write it — "1 Ин" and "1Ин", "1Иоанна" and "1 Иоанна", "И.Нав" — is one rule, `getBookByWrittenName(word, locale?)` in `frontend/app/utils/bibleBooks.ts` (case, spaces and dots do not count). Pass the writer's language wherever it is known — `parseReferenceText(raw, { locale })`: "1Цар" is 1 Samuel in Russian (1 Царств) and 1 Kings in Ukrainian (1 Царів); with no language the Russian reading stays. The parser's own alias table (`ORDERED_BOOKS`) still holds spoken forms the book module lacks; it is a second table, not to be grown with spellings the rule already reads.
- Search: `scriptureReferenceSearchText(ref, locale)` — the short and long forms lowercased, psalms numbered as displayed — so a reference read off a card finds that card.
- AI prompts ask for book names in English (`frontend/app/api/clients/studyNote.structured.ts`): the stored `book` is the canonical English id the parser and the book table key on.
- Dictated references ("Deuteronomy 10 chapter 11 verse"): prompt wording alone does not hold. `normalizeSpokenScriptureReferences` (`frontend/app/utils/scriptureReferenceNormalizer.ts`) is a deterministic pass after the model that reuses `parseReferenceText` and writes the citation form; it runs on polish and thought outputs (`polishTranscription.structured.ts`, `thought.structured.ts`) and is tested in `frontend/__tests__/utils/scriptureReferenceNormalizer.test.ts` and both client tests. Speech goes the other way: `normalizeScriptureReferencesForTts`.
- Finding books inside free text (`extractBookIdsFromVerse` in `frontend/app/components/calendar/calendarAnalytics.ts`): a fuzzy prefix match only for 2–4-character tokens (`isFuzzyAbbrevMatch`), longer words must share at least 5 leading characters (`isPrefixInflectionMatch`), and a hit counts only when a number or a chapter/verse word follows — or the text is the book alone. Otherwise ordinary words become books.

- Find every reference a text names (a meeting block's text, a note body) with `referencesInText(text, locale)` in `bibleLink.ts`: the name must read as a book by itself and be followed only by numbers; a chapter alone followed by a word ("Даниил 3 года") is a count, unless the word joins a list ("Рим 13 и Еф 6"). Each result has `start`/`end` in the text, `raw` as written and the `reference` in the interface's Bible.
- Show a passage over the current screen with `components/bible/VerseSheet.tsx` (`reference`, `onClose`, `keepVisible` top/bottom refs): it reads the device's Bible and maps the numbers itself — a screen never renumbers.
- A study note's reading-mode chip opens `components/bible/ScripturePreviewSheet.tsx`, which composes the existing `VerseSheet` with an optional `related` panel. Text and personal notes remain mounted at the same size, with the inactive panel invisible and inert, so the book's reading anchor and the notes' list position survive switching. `useScriptureUsage` reads owner-scoped study notes through the engine; `utils/scriptureUsage.ts` finds explicit, overlapping uses. A row is the note's strongest link (same reference → overlapping passage → mention in the text, newer first), with a plain-text excerpt (`markdownPreviewText` in `utils/markdownUtils.ts`) around the mention of the opened passage. A note opens whole through `FoldableMarkdown` in `ReaderSheet` above the Bible (z-[260]), centred on that mention and painted through the CSS Custom Highlight API (`utils/cssHighlights.ts`, shared with Bible marks); a reference split by a Markdown hard line break is not found and the note opens at the top. Back to the verse selects the original text panel; «Open the note» navigates in the same window (a new tab would leave the installed app). Edit-mode reference picking stays separate. Incomplete lists are labelled. The broader sermon/group overview remains a case backlog item.

- Make the references in a screen's text links: wrap the text in `<ScriptureLinks onOpen={…}>` (`components/bible/ScriptureLinks.tsx`). `MarkdownDisplay` and `PlanMarkdown` link references only inside it, `ScriptureText` does the same for a plain string, and `WithoutScriptureLinks` turns it off for a document drawn on such a page (a PDF). `onOpen` decides the press: `setVerse` for the verse window (a meeting, a sermon being preached), or `useOpenInBible().openReference` to go to the Bible with the way back (saves first through `beforeSwitch`). A copied plan turns the links back into words (`copyFormattedFromElement`).

## Traps

- Three note searches still build reference text by hand instead of `scriptureReferenceSearchText`: the `filteredNotes` filter in `studies/page.tsx` (it runs before `matchesSearchTokens`, so it can drop a note the card-based match would find), `useFilteredNotes` in `studies/[id]/page.tsx`, and the search in `frontend/app/services/studies.service.ts` (English names). None of them knows the abbreviation or the psalm renumbering.
- `parseReferenceText` accepts a prefix of any length of an English book id and a book with no chapter. Right for the picker where a person types a reference; wrong for scanning prose — use the free-text rules above.

## Why

- 2026-10-17: old references are read in the author's interface language, on the screen and on the server (the owner's rule). Guessing by the note's letters read a Ukrainian title with none of "і, ї, є, ґ" as Russian (BUG-20261017-old-uk-note-read-as-russian), and the server, which knew no language, read such a note otherwise than its author's screen (BUG-20261017-server-reads-note-without-author-language).
- 2026-10-17: BUG-20261017-reference-across-chapters-in-other-bible and BUG-20261017-old-uk-note-read-as-russian, found when item 4.1 was accepted. A reference whose verses fell in two chapters of another Bible printed as the whole chapters ("Ps 9-10", 38 verses for 6) or lost the chapter holding fewer (Rom 16:24), the picker saved what it showed, and a Ukrainian title with none of "і, ї, є, ґ" read as Russian. Passages carry what the stored shape cannot say.
- 2026-10-15: BUG-20261015-spaced-numbered-abbreviation-not-read. 53 written forms read as nothing or the wrong book: "1 Ин 4:8" with a space, "1Иоанна" glued, "И.Нав", and Ukrainian "1 Цар" as 1 Samuel.
- 2026-10-14: BUG-20261012-psalm-verse-numbering-not-mapped. The parser, the picker and the AI moved a Russian Psalm's chapter by one and left the verse, so "Пс 50:12" was stored as Ps 51:12 and an English screen and the AI read the neighbouring verse ("Restore unto me the joy" instead of "Create in me a clean heart"). The owner asked for one module that maps by Bible, not by language.
- 2026-09-16: eight builders were collapsed into this one; the calendar had borrowed a raw English one and printed "Luke 5:17-26" under a note whose own chips showed the Russian abbreviation.
- 2026-04-25: prompt wording alone did not turn dictated references into citation notation; a deterministic pass did.
- 2026-02-02: fuzzy prefix matching on long tokens and on text without a chapter number produced false book matches.
