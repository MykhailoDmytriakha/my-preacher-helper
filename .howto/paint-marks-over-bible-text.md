# Paint marks over the Bible text

The marks (`frontend/app/components/bible/marks/`, rules in `frontend/app/utils/bibleMarks.ts`) are painted over the words, never by wrapping words in elements: a ribbon of the Psalms is tens of thousands of words. The pencil's line is the browser's own highlight; the marker is rounded bands of one height drawn behind each verse's row (since 2026-11-02).

## How

- **Rules first, in one module.** `utils/bibleMarks.ts` owns marks: runs of word positions per verse, two layers (marker colour, pencil), a stroke cut at each verse edge, "the same colour over it erases", the eraser takes both. Screens never compute marks.
- **Words are counted one way.** A mark's n-th word is `verseWords(raw).split(/\s+/)` (`countVerseWords`); on the page `verseWordDom.ts` walks `[data-verse-text]` of `[data-verse="c:v"]` counting a `<br>` as a space. Change one and the stored marks land on other words.
- **Paint — the pencil:** one `Highlight` per pencil colour registered once (`CSS.highlights.set('bible-mark-pencil-<colour>', …)`, `useMarkHighlights.ts`), and only the ranges of verses whose marks changed are `delete`d and `add`ed — WebKit repaints every range of a highlight replaced whole (90 marks in the Psalms: 67 ms a frame against 34 for one range added). Safari < 17.2 has no `CSS.highlights`: the reader then shows no tools (`marksSupported`).
- **Paint — the marker** (the owner, 2026-11-02: `::highlight` cannot round a corner, and his iPad drew it taller on some lines): `markBands.ts` measures each marker run's boxes (`Range.getClientRects`), joins the pieces of a line, and draws one band per line — the same height on every line (1.32 of the text size), centred on the words, ends rounded — as ONE SVG picture in the verse row's own `background-image`. Nothing is put into the page (an element inserted inside `#app-shell` restyles 69 000 nodes in Chrome), and coordinates are against the row, so a comment opened above or chapters coming and going move nothing. The row paints its background above its `::before`/`::after` tints (z-index −10) and under its text; the pencil's highlight lies over the band. A `ResizeObserver` on each painted row measures it again (width, text size, a font arriving); a `MutationObserver` on `<html class>` repaints on a theme change. Colours: `--mark-fill-<colour>` in `globals.css` (`:root` light, `:root.dark` dark), read when the bands are drawn — the same variables every element fill uses (`markPaint.ts` `MARK_FILL_CLASS`: the legend, the analysis words, the window of a verse marked elsewhere, where a run of words is one `box-decoration-break: clone` band). Inside `url("data:image/svg+xml,…")` escape `'` and brackets too: jsdom drops the value otherwise, and a parser may end the url early. Tests: jsdom lays nothing out — give `getBoundingClientRect` and `Range.prototype.getClientRects` (`markBands.test.ts`, `markHighlights.test.tsx`; `fillsPaintedOn(row)` says what a row holds).
- **Word under a point:** `caretPositionFromPoint`, else `caretRangeFromPoint`, else the word boxes (`getClientRects`) of the row under the point. Do not put `user-select: none` on the text — it can blind the caret APIs; stop selection with `selectstart` instead.
- **Gestures** (`useMarkStrokes.ts`): pointer events on the article. Where marks can be made the text carries `touch-action: pan-y pinch-zoom` and `-webkit-touch-callout: none`, never switched with the tool (a finger paints along a line, scrolls up and down, pinches; a scroll arrives as `pointercancel` and the stroke is dropped) — only while the page is pinched bigger (`utils/pageZoom.ts`, the same rule the swipe back uses) with no tool in hand does `pan-y` go, so the sideways pull pans to the clipped side (Codex's review); a tool in hand only adds `data-gesture-ignore` (the app's swipe back, `navigation/gestures/touchTargets.ts`, stays off) — an attribute no style reads. A mouse's crosshair is an inline `cursor` on the hovered verse row. An Apple Pencil is `pointerType === 'pen'`: it paints with the last tool, nothing taken, and its `touchmove` (`touchType === 'stylus'`) is `preventDefault`ed while a stroke is on.
- **Hover on the tools** answers a mouse, a trackpad and an Apple Pencil held over the glass, never a finger (`utils/pointerHover.ts` → `data-hover`, styled `data-[hover]:`): `(hover: hover)` says "no" on an iPad even with a Pencil, and a plain `hover:` stays on after a finger's tap.
- **The colours are out exactly while the marker or the pencil is in hand** (the owner, 2026-10-16): open colours are how he sees the tool is taken. A colour picked stays in hand with its colours out; pressing the tool again, or Escape, puts it away; a press elsewhere (a stroke) closes nothing. `palette` is derived from `inHand` (`markTool.ts`). Both palettes stay mounted and are only `hidden` (`MarkToolbar.tsx`). Two copies of the toolbar share one state on a phone (the line's, scrolled away, and the column over the "T"). Over another window — the passage analysis lends the reader's tools (`useReaderMarks.analysisMarks`) — the Escape that puts a tool away is the toolbar's alone: it stops it (`stopPropagation`), the toolbar answers only when `keyboardReaches(element)` from the existing `utils/modalLayerRegistry.ts` says it belongs to the top window and no newer external dialog has focus. The shared `useModalLayer` closes the window on a separate eligible Escape; keep that ownership in the existing registry. Without that, the window re-subscribed its listener on the toolbar's re-render and took the same press: Escape with the marker in hand closed the analysis (2026-10-30, `marks.test.tsx`).
- **Storage — every device:** `markStore.ts` keeps a book's marks in the data engine (`bibleMarks`, one document per person, Bible and book under the fixed id `<uid>_<bible>_<book-slug>`, written only by the engine — `firestore.rules` lets the owner read, the browser never write; `rules-test-closed.cjs`). Verses are a map (`v26_4`), so strokes in different verses from two devices merge, and a verse's marker and pencil too. A verse layer is ONE string of runs `first-last-colour` joined by commas (`encodeBookMarks` / `decodeBookMarks`), never a list: the engine merges a list of strings as a set, and a verse recoloured blue on one device and green on the other kept both colours on the same words (probe through `mergeFields`, 2026-10-16); one string is one value. The engine merges a layer word by word (`utils/bibleMarkLayers.ts` `mergeVerseLayer`, wired in `data-engine/protocol.ts` `mergeFields` for `verses.v<c>_<v>` and its `m`/`p`): strokes on different words of one verse from two devices keep each other, and only a word changed differently on both sides is a question; a verse absent on one side is a verse without marks (BUG-20261107-marks-same-verse-offline-conflict — the owner's iPad, offline strokes in a verse that already had marks). Firestore keeps no array inside an array either. A stroke reaches the engine as a change of the newest copy (`commit(resource, current => …)`), never as a value computed from an older one — and `commit` also makes the book's document where the engine's own read finds none, so a list not read yet never decides "create" (that create is refused once another device made the book). Offline in a book this device has no copy of, `needsConnectionFailure` → `create`. The tools wait for the list (`!collection.loading`). A live check across two devices: `localhost` and `127.0.0.1` are two origins with two engine stores; override `visibilityState` in the automation tab or the engine never reads (`use-data-engine-in-a-screen.md`).
- **Pencil colours:** the pencil line is grey or one of the five (`PENCIL_COLORS`); a line saved before the pencil had colours reads grey. One `::highlight(bible-mark-pencil-<colour>)` per colour.
- **A Pencil's hold is the verse's** (the owner, 2026-11-02): held still on a verse for `LONG_PRESS_MS` — a tool in hand or not — the stroke its press began is dropped (`useMarkStrokes` returns `takeForHold(pointerId)`; a stroke that reached another word stays a stroke) and the verse's menu opens on the lift like a finger's. The decision is made synchronously in the hold's timer (`useLongPress` `onArm`): a decision through a React effect let a lift right after save the dot first (Codex). A tool in hand is put away (`useReaderMarks().penHold`). A finger's or a Pencil's press never starts the system's selection: `VerseHoldSurface` cancels `selectstart` after such a press and clears a selection that comes anyway (one `verse-pick` line `selection-stopped` / `selection-cleared` per press); the menu's layer is `select-none` — the lifted verse stands outside the text's card, and the owner's iPad put the selection handle on it. Not by `(hover: none)`: an iPad with a trackpad says it can hover.
- **Marks made in another Bible** (2.9.3): they lie on that translation's words, so here a verse carries only a sign — a thin stripe of their colours in the text's margin, outside the row's flow (`BookRibbon` `signs`). It stands at the verse of THIS Bible that is the same place (`placesIn`, `utils/versification`: Synodal Ps 22 = King James Ps 23), and a tap opens the verse there whole, painted as there (`marks/MarkedElsewhereSheet.tsx`, drawn as elements with `markPaint.ts` — the colours `globals.css` paints over the reader, kept in step by hand). Signs and the window come from one list (`markedElsewhere` → `signsHere` / `versesElsewhere`), so a sign never opens onto nothing. The other Bibles' marks come from the same `bibleMarks` list, keyed by value (`othersKey`): the list comes anew with every stroke, and an array key would redraw the whole book's ribbon on each one. "Open in this translation" closes the window first and changes the Bible a frame later (`useFollowAfterClose`): on iOS a window pins the page, and a place written down while pinned is not the one read.
- **An unseen book started offline:** the canonical commit queue retains the captured private draft without preparing a command until `engine.readWriteTarget` can read that fixed book freshly (`{ fresh: true }`, also while the page is hidden). An absent target uses ordinary create; a live matching book uses ordinary update with `bibleMarksCreation.firstBookMarksIntent`: a logical empty book, with empty objects only at shared verse keys, lets the existing server merger keep independent verses and marker/pencil layers. The layer merge is by words: different words of the same layer combine; the same word changed differently on both sides stays an explicit conflict; a whole verse erased there with another layer drawn here combines too. Do not decode/re-encode stored layer values during this merge or invent an upsert for generic create. The command uses the target generation, preserves its creation metadata, and retains the ORIGINAL private draft as `submittedValue`, so ACK projection keeps remote siblings and later unsent strokes. A tombstone is never recreated. Already prepared or unknown commands are immutable. For an older terminal create refusal, explicit KeepMine fresh-reads before retiring the receipt, then the same first-book rule preserves the other verses/layers. Regressions: `data-engine/__tests__/offlineMarksCreation.test.ts`.
- **One recovery decision per held book:** the global `EngineConflictBanner` yields only when the current book address matches AND the engine's exact owner/resource/request-ID/draft proof says its reader holds ALL the answered work. `PrivateChrome` supplies its existing search context online and offline; `/bible` alone, another book, an explicit different `read`, or unseen work never hides the notice. Outside the reader, `documentScreenHref(resource, value)` validates the canonical marks ID against saved owner/Bible/book metadata instead of parsing the owner's ID. The canonical `bibleHref` option `readingBible` writes `read`: an explicit word-level translation arrival, distinct from `tr` reference numbering. `useReadingBible` applies it once and a later picker choice consumes `read`. The explicit recovery visit passes `decideMarksOnArrival`: ordinary initial verse restoration stays out of that visit, and once text, pending choice and measured nav are ready the existing status is exposed once by `revealBelow`. The helper reuses `scrollPinnedPageBy` while iOS has pinned the page and otherwise scrolls the normal window; passive renders do not pull later reading back. Marks recovery uses a native document link so revisiting the same URL after another manual translation creates a fresh reader; ordinary recovery links retain their existing navigation. `bibleMarksRecoveryValue` labels the stored translation/book/verse, marker/pencil meaning and one-based word range. `decodeCompleteBookMarks` uses the existing decoder/encoder to prove completeness, including complete legacy string arrays; unknown fields or malformed layers stay visible in the existing fallback. This display never normalizes or writes the stored document. Regressions: `EngineConflictBannerBibleMarks.test.tsx`, `bibleMarksRecovery.test.ts`, `useReadingBible.test.tsx`, `bibleLink.test.ts`.
- **One form per book** (`markStore.ts`): the book's document is edited through ONE shared engine form (`useDataDocument`, slot `bible-marks`, `create` settled once when the bibleMarks list is read whole and lacks the document); every stroke is `form.commit(updater)` at once, kept on the device before the network is asked, and rapid strokes chain (engine fix `controller.ts` ~164: the predecessor is chosen when the save runs). One-shot `useDocumentActions().commit` per stroke opened a new editor each time on the same copy — the server answered `conflict` to the person's own strokes (owner's iPad, 2026-10-16); a custom serial writer then left strokes in page memory during a delivery. Strokes are taken only while the form can write (`writable`); while it is a read-only copy (silent storage after an iPad wakes) the marks show and the reader says to wait. Do NOT auto-`recover` closed drafts on open: the source is not retired, every open recovers again and the form blinks (tried 2026-10-16; BUG-20261016-engine-draft-before-request).
- **First offline book:** check the stored owner, Bible and fixed marks document ID. A display label and its slug can represent the same book. Keep the stored label while merging marks.
- **Where a stroke may start:** only on a verse's row, never on what is pressed there (`button, a, [role=button]…`) — a Pencil's tap on a chip opens the links. **The Pencil takes nothing:** it paints with the last tool, a finger paints only with a tool it took (the black ring came and went, and a finger after the Pencil painted); the Pencil's tool wears a soft grey ring (`penTool`). The text with marks is a `data-pen-surface`: a Pencil there never starts the page's swipe back (`usePageGestures`).
- **No React per word:** the stroke being drawn is painted by the highlights directly (`useMarkHighlights` returns a `MarkPainter`: `draw`, `release`, `drop`, `shown`), and the word under a moving pointer is looked up once a frame — the reader redrawing on every word made an iPad lag. **A stroke let go stays drawn until its save arrives:** the form hands new data ~100 ms after a commit (after the device write), and repainting the stored marks at once made the stroke blink out and back. Kept strokes are held as changes (a stroke applied twice changes nothing more) over the stored marks and let go from the OLDEST while the stored marks hold them (`marksKey(change(marks)) === marksKey(marks)`) — never each on its own: strokes over the same words must stay in their order (Codex: an eraser held past the red stroke after it hid the saved red). A stroke that changes nothing shown is not held at all (an eraser over bare words would hide a mark arriving from another device). At most 3 s (the person took the server's version; the engine publishes the draft before the device write, `engine.ts` ~849, so a save arrives far sooner), dropped on a failed save (`save(change, lost)`) and on a new text; what the painter reads is published in a layout effect, never while React renders. A new stroke starts from `painter.shown()`, held strokes included.
- **Speed in a big book (owner's iPad, 2026-10-16; measured in Playwright WebKit and Chrome, production build, the Psalms = 20 536 elements):** anything that restyles the whole text costs hundreds of milliseconds a tap. (1) A class switched on the article with the tool: `touch-action` 500 ms, `cursor` 280 ms in WebKit — so the text's manner is static. (2) An element put into or taken out of the page anywhere inside `#app-shell`: Chrome re-checks the shell's `:has([data-reader-panes])` rules (`globals.css`) and restyles all 69 000 nodes, 150 ms — so palettes stay mounted and hidden; any frequent open/close in the reader must toggle, not mount. (3) The same marks repainted at each of a save's ~16 steps: `markStore.ts` keeps one marks object while `marksKey` holds, and the painter skips an unchanged value. Before → after, WebKit Psalms: tap 506–544 → 16–25 ms; Chrome: 174–184 → 10–23 ms. How to measure: `measure-typing-lag.md` → "Taps and strokes".
- **Phone:** the tools stand in a capsule at the bottom centre — marker, pencil, eraser and the text menu's «T» (`PhoneReaderTools.tsx` → `MarkCapsule` in `MarkToolbar.tsx`); the colours of the tool in hand float over it as a row of dots, 44 px targets with a halo of the page's colour, hidden by the `hidden` attribute while the «T» card is open (a `contents` class would outweigh `[hidden]`). The owner chose it on the canvas, 2026-11-07 (K4): the earlier column at the right edge covered the end of every line while marking. Without marks (signed out, Safari < 17.2) a round «T» stands at the bottom right. The «T» card is made at its first opening and kept mounted, so a translation picker opened from it can finish. The line keeps the tools only from `md` (owner's Android, 2026-10-16: the line grew to three rows).
- **Technical details:** each stroke's end is a `mark` event — `source` = pen / touch / mouse, `result` = kept / cancelled / missed, and the writer's `waiting` / `refused` with `code`. Read them in the owner's feedback report before guessing what an iPad did.

## Visit an analysis in its source Bible

An analysis deep link supplies its own word-level Bible. Reuse `useReadingBible(locale, requestedBible, { persistRequested: false })` for that visit; the request key includes the Bible and persistence policy and is consumed once, so the retained address cannot undo a later manual picker choice. A normal explicit recovery arrival retains the default persistent behavior. Never implement a second localStorage writer in the analysis route. Source-frame checks still guard the note independently of the displayed Bible. The canonical `analysisState` and `storedAnalysis` in `utils/passageAnalysis.ts` carry nonempty `drawn` both ways; empty optional state preserves the old stored shape.
