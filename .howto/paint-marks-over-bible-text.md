# Paint marks over the Bible text

The marks (`frontend/app/components/bible/marks/`, rules in `frontend/app/utils/bibleMarks.ts`) are painted by the browser over the words, never by wrapping words in elements: a ribbon of the Psalms is tens of thousands of words.

## How

- **Rules first, in one module.** `utils/bibleMarks.ts` owns marks: runs of word positions per verse, two layers (marker colour, pencil), a stroke cut at each verse edge, "the same colour over it erases", the eraser takes both. Screens never compute marks.
- **Words are counted one way.** A mark's n-th word is `verseWords(raw).split(/\s+/)` (`countVerseWords`); on the page `verseWordDom.ts` walks `[data-verse-text]` of `[data-verse="c:v"]` counting a `<br>` as a space. Change one and the stored marks land on other words.
- **Paint:** one `Highlight` per colour registered once (`CSS.highlights.set('bible-mark-<colour>', …)`, `useMarkHighlights.ts`), and only the ranges of verses whose marks changed are `delete`d and `add`ed — WebKit repaints every range of a highlight replaced whole (90 marks in the Psalms: 67 ms a frame against 34 for one range added). Colours in `globals.css` `::highlight(bible-mark-…)` with `:root.dark` pairs. Safari < 17.2 has no `CSS.highlights`: the reader then shows no tools (`marksSupported`).
- **Word under a point:** `caretPositionFromPoint`, else `caretRangeFromPoint`, else the word boxes (`getClientRects`) of the row under the point. Do not put `user-select: none` on the text — it can blind the caret APIs; stop selection with `selectstart` instead.
- **Gestures** (`useMarkStrokes.ts`): pointer events on the article. Where marks can be made the text carries `touch-action: pan-y pinch-zoom` and `-webkit-touch-callout: none`, never switched with the tool (a finger paints along a line, scrolls up and down, pinches; a scroll arrives as `pointercancel` and the stroke is dropped) — only while the page is pinched bigger (`utils/pageZoom.ts`, the same rule the swipe back uses) with no tool in hand does `pan-y` go, so the sideways pull pans to the clipped side (Codex's review); a tool in hand only adds `data-gesture-ignore` (the app's swipe back, `navigation/gestures/touchTargets.ts`, stays off) — an attribute no style reads. A mouse's crosshair is an inline `cursor` on the hovered verse row. An Apple Pencil is `pointerType === 'pen'`: it paints with the last tool, nothing taken, and its `touchmove` (`touchType === 'stylus'`) is `preventDefault`ed while a stroke is on.
- **Hover on the tools** answers a mouse, a trackpad and an Apple Pencil held over the glass, never a finger (`utils/pointerHover.ts` → `data-hover`, styled `data-[hover]:`): `(hover: hover)` says "no" on an iPad even with a Pencil, and a plain `hover:` stays on after a finger's tap.
- **The colours are out exactly while the marker or the pencil is in hand** (the owner, 2026-10-16): open colours are how he sees the tool is taken. A colour picked stays in hand with its colours out; pressing the tool again, or Escape, puts it away; a press elsewhere (a stroke) closes nothing. `palette` is derived from `inHand` (`markTool.ts`). Both palettes stay mounted and are only `hidden` (`MarkToolbar.tsx`). Two copies of the toolbar share one state on a phone (the line's, scrolled away, and the column over the "T").
- **Storage — every device:** `markStore.ts` keeps a book's marks in the data engine (`bibleMarks`, one document per person, Bible and book under the fixed id `<uid>_<bible>_<book-slug>`, written only by the engine — `firestore.rules` lets the owner read, the browser never write; `rules-test-closed.cjs`). Verses are a map (`v26_4`), so strokes in different verses from two devices merge, and a verse's marker and pencil too. A verse layer is ONE string of runs `first-last-colour` joined by commas (`encodeBookMarks` / `decodeBookMarks`), never a list: the engine merges a list of strings as a set, and a verse recoloured blue on one device and green on the other kept both colours on the same words (probe through `mergeFields`, 2026-10-16); one string is one value, so the same verse from two devices is a question. Firestore keeps no array inside an array either. A stroke reaches the engine as a change of the newest copy (`commit(resource, current => …)`), never as a value computed from an older one — and `commit` also makes the book's document where the engine's own read finds none, so a list not read yet never decides "create" (that create is refused once another device made the book). Offline in a book this device has no copy of, `needsConnectionFailure` → `create`. The tools wait for the list (`!collection.loading`). A live check across two devices: `localhost` and `127.0.0.1` are two origins with two engine stores; override `visibilityState` in the automation tab or the engine never reads (`use-data-engine-in-a-screen.md`).
- **Pencil colours:** the pencil line is grey or one of the five (`PENCIL_COLORS`); a line saved before the pencil had colours reads grey. One `::highlight(bible-mark-pencil-<colour>)` per colour.
- **Marks made in another Bible** (2.9.3): they lie on that translation's words, so here a verse carries only a sign — a thin stripe of their colours in the text's margin, outside the row's flow (`BookRibbon` `signs`). It stands at the verse of THIS Bible that is the same place (`placesIn`, `utils/versification`: Synodal Ps 22 = King James Ps 23), and a tap opens the verse there whole, painted as there (`marks/MarkedElsewhereSheet.tsx`, drawn as elements with `markPaint.ts` — the colours `globals.css` paints over the reader, kept in step by hand). Signs and the window come from one list (`markedElsewhere` → `signsHere` / `versesElsewhere`), so a sign never opens onto nothing. The other Bibles' marks come from the same `bibleMarks` list, keyed by value (`othersKey`): the list comes anew with every stroke, and an array key would redraw the whole book's ribbon on each one. "Open in this translation" closes the window first and changes the Bible a frame later (`useFollowAfterClose`): on iOS a window pins the page, and a place written down while pinned is not the one read.
- **One form per book** (`markStore.ts`): the book's document is edited through ONE shared engine form (`useDataDocument`, slot `bible-marks`, `create` settled once when the bibleMarks list is read whole and lacks the document); every stroke is `form.commit(updater)` at once, kept on the device before the network is asked, and rapid strokes chain (engine fix `controller.ts` ~164: the predecessor is chosen when the save runs). One-shot `useDocumentActions().commit` per stroke opened a new editor each time on the same copy — the server answered `conflict` to the person's own strokes (owner's iPad, 2026-10-16); a custom serial writer then left strokes in page memory during a delivery. Strokes are taken only while the form can write (`writable`); while it is a read-only copy (silent storage after an iPad wakes) the marks show and the reader says to wait. Do NOT auto-`recover` closed drafts on open: the source is not retired, every open recovers again and the form blinks (tried 2026-10-16; BUG-20261016-engine-draft-before-request).
- **Where a stroke may start:** only on a verse's row, never on what is pressed there (`button, a, [role=button]…`) — a Pencil's tap on a chip opens the links. **The Pencil takes nothing:** it paints with the last tool, a finger paints only with a tool it took (the black ring came and went, and a finger after the Pencil painted); the Pencil's tool wears a soft grey ring (`penTool`). The text with marks is a `data-pen-surface`: a Pencil there never starts the page's swipe back (`usePageGestures`).
- **No React per word:** the stroke being drawn is painted by the highlights directly (`useMarkHighlights` returns a `MarkPainter`: `draw`, `release`, `drop`, `shown`), and the word under a moving pointer is looked up once a frame — the reader redrawing on every word made an iPad lag. **A stroke let go stays drawn until its save arrives:** the form hands new data ~100 ms after a commit (after the device write), and repainting the stored marks at once made the stroke blink out and back. Kept strokes are held as changes (a stroke applied twice changes nothing more) over the stored marks and let go from the OLDEST while the stored marks hold them (`marksKey(change(marks)) === marksKey(marks)`) — never each on its own: strokes over the same words must stay in their order (Codex: an eraser held past the red stroke after it hid the saved red). A stroke that changes nothing shown is not held at all (an eraser over bare words would hide a mark arriving from another device). At most 3 s (the person took the server's version; the engine publishes the draft before the device write, `engine.ts` ~849, so a save arrives far sooner), dropped on a failed save (`save(change, lost)`) and on a new text; what the painter reads is published in a layout effect, never while React renders. A new stroke starts from `painter.shown()`, held strokes included.
- **Speed in a big book (owner's iPad, 2026-10-16; measured in Playwright WebKit and Chrome, production build, the Psalms = 20 536 elements):** anything that restyles the whole text costs hundreds of milliseconds a tap. (1) A class switched on the article with the tool: `touch-action` 500 ms, `cursor` 280 ms in WebKit — so the text's manner is static. (2) An element put into or taken out of the page anywhere inside `#app-shell`: Chrome re-checks the shell's `:has([data-reader-panes])` rules (`globals.css`) and restyles all 69 000 nodes, 150 ms — so palettes stay mounted and hidden; any frequent open/close in the reader must toggle, not mount. (3) The same marks repainted at each of a save's ~16 steps: `markStore.ts` keeps one marks object while `marksKey` holds, and the painter skips an unchanged value. Before → after, WebKit Psalms: tap 506–544 → 16–25 ms; Chrome: 174–184 → 10–23 ms. How to measure: `measure-typing-lag.md` → "Taps and strokes".
- **Phone:** the tools always stand in the column over the "T" (`floatingTools`, `md:hidden`); the line keeps them only from `md` (owner's Android, 2026-10-16: the line grew to three rows, and the tools vanished between the line scrolling away and the column appearing).
- **Technical details:** each stroke's end is a `mark` event — `source` = pen / touch / mouse, `result` = kept / cancelled / missed, and the writer's `waiting` / `refused` with `code`. Read them in the owner's feedback report before guessing what an iPad did.
