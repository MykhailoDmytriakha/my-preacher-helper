# Show and rebuild the Bible's cross references

A verse of the reader carries a chip with the number of its main links; the chip or the verse number opens the window of its links, and "Open" goes to the passage with the way back to the verse. The data is ONE set made from fourteen MyBible modules (the owner, 2026-10-15: "a set, not a list"), one file per book in `frontend/public/bible-crossrefs/<version>/` (the version is `BIBLE_CROSSREFS_VERSION` in `app/utils/bibleCrossRefsCache.ts`, v2 since 2026-10-16), kept in the King James numbering.

## How

- A screen never reads the set itself. `frontend/app/utils/crossReferences.ts` answers "this verse, in this Bible → its links, as places of the same Bible": `crossReferencesAt(set, bible, place)`, `countCrossReferences` for the chips, `crossReferenceLabel` for names, `loadCrossRefs(bookId)` to fetch a book's file. Numbering moves only through `utils/versification` (a link to KJV Ps 18:10–12 is Synodal Ps 17:11–13).
- A screen that draws a book with `BookRibbon` gets everything from `useBookCrossRefs(bookId, bible, text, 'reader' | 'window')` (`frontend/app/components/bible/useBookCrossRefs.ts`): pass `ribbon` to `<BookRibbon crossRefs>` and render `<CrossReferenceSheet>` with `openAt`, `set`, `close`. The reader (`BibleBookReader`) and the passage window (`VerseSheet`) are the two users.
- What shows is the person's choice on the device, `crossReferencePrefs.ts` (`show`, `inWindow`, `mode` main/all, `othersAsVerses`), set in Settings → Bible (`/settings/bible`, `components/settings/CrossReferenceSettings.tsx`) — not in the reader's "T", which is about the text (the owner, 2026-10-20). A passage window shows chips only with `inWindow`. The choice is a `utils/deviceChoice.ts` store: held in the page's memory, never re-read from storage to draw (`.howto/keep-a-device-choice.md`).
- The window of a verse's links (`CrossReferenceSheet`) shows the main links with their WHOLE text (never a line clamp — the owner's iPad, 2026-10-16) and folds the rest; opened, the rest's row carries a switch before its chevron: on, the rest read as verses with their text in the Bible's order (their books' texts fetched only then); off, as references by book. The switch is `othersAsVerses`, the same one as in Settings → Bible, and stands BESIDE the row's button and its chevron, never laid over them: a 24 px switch over the 44 px row made a finger a few pixels off fold the rest instead (the owner's iPad, 2026-10-16). Every change of these settings goes to the feedback report's technical details (`setting` — from the window, the settings or another tab; to what; kept on the device or only in memory). The window is `ReaderSheet`: 768 px on a tablet, a 496 px card on a wide screen (`xl`; a tenth wider since 2026-10-20 — the owner: wider on the computer, the tablet keeps its width). One link opens as a SECOND `ReaderSheet` over the list (`depth` 2, a layer above — `linkLayerClassName`, z-[130] over the reader, z-[270] over a passage window): a cascade, narrower and lower, the list showing around it (on a wide screen 456 px inside the list's 496 — widen one, widen the other, or the card sticks out past the list); closing it (✕, Escape, the dimmed area) finds the list exactly as it was — scroll, the rest open, the switch (the owner, 2026-10-16: the link used to replace the list, and going back the list started over).
- That card shows the link in its context — its WHOLE chapter and only that chapter (`ReferenceInContext`; the owner, 2026-10-19: "it must be seen that the context is one chapter"): the chapter's name and verse count above the text, "end of chapter N" below it, the passage lit and brought to the middle, "back to the verse" when scrolled away (`verseSheetGeometry`), "Open" to go there and read on. A link to a whole chapter lights nothing and opens at its start. The rows are the ribbon's own (`ChapterVerses` in `BookRibbon.tsx`) but detached — no `data-verse`, no `data-verse-text` — so the reader's marks and its place read never take them for the book being read. The card is `ReaderSheet` with `fill`: full height, its own pane scrolling between the head and "Open". The pane's height follows the screen (a tablet turned): not scrolled, the passage rests in the middle again; scrolled, "back to the verse" is asked again (a `ResizeObserver` on the pane — scroll alone never told it).
- How many sources give a link is not written anywhere on screen: the owner did not read "5 independent sources" (2026-10-19), and main / the rest already say it. The weight decides only main.
- "Open" starts reading there; the way back ("← Ис 19") returns to the verse, not to the list of its links — going back into the list waits for the history of places (case `2026-10-12-block-diagram`, L7).
- Main = at least three INDEPENDENT sources (`MAIN_REFERENCE_WEIGHT`). The weight is counted by families: TSK, TSK2 and OpenBible are one voice (OpenBible is 97% TSK), Pa and PP one, the Slovak Jerusalem Bible and the Studium Biblicum one.
- Rebuild the set from the modules (kept outside the repo, `~/MyProjects/bible-modules/crossreferences`): from `frontend/`, `node scripts/build-bible-crossrefs.mjs <modules folder> public/bible-crossrefs/v3`, change `BIBLE_CROSSREFS_VERSION` to `v3` and remove the old folder. The script refuses a folder that is not empty: a published set is never rewritten, the service worker keeps it forever (`app/sw.ts`, its own CacheFirst rule and cache `bible-crossrefs`).
- The merge rule lives in the script only: inside one target chapter a range within another melts into it (1–4 takes 2–3), overlapping ranges join, touching ranges stay apart; modules marked `requires_reverse_processing` get the other direction added; `russian_numbering` modules are moved to KJV first.

## Traps

- The set loads AFTER the book's text (`useBookCrossRefs`): fetched first, it took the reader's failed-network answer in `BibleBookReader.test` and the text loaded instead of the error — and on a weak network the text must not wait behind the links.
- Counting the chips moves ~150 000 places for the Psalter; `crossReferences.ts` remembers moved places (130 → 71 ms on a computer). Do not call `placesIn` per link elsewhere.
- The reader's text follows `--text-scale` now; a size change keeps the verse being read at the top (`useKeepPlaceOnTextScale` — the provider writes the size in its own effect, after the reader's, so the jump waits a frame).
- The light of a lit verse reaches 6 px past its row on every side (`ARRIVAL_CLASSES`): a scrolling pane that holds lit rows needs that room, or it scrolls sideways — a scrollbar under the verse on a computer, a finger moving the text on a tablet (the owner, 2026-10-20). The reader and the passage window have side padding; the link card's pane reaches 6 px into the card's margin and gives it back as padding (`-inset-x-1.5 px-1.5`), so its text kept its width. Measure `scrollWidth - clientWidth` of the pane, not a screenshot: it was 6 on every width.
- `CrossReferenceSheet` over the passage window (`z-[260]` over `z-[250]`) has its own focus trap; the window's trap yields to the topmost layer.
- "Open" moves the reader only after the window has closed (`useFollowAfterClose`, a frame later): on iOS the window pins the page and, released, scrolls it back — a jump made under it was undone. A desktop browser does not show this; only iOS does.
- A passage always ends inside its chapter. The modules write "Isaiah 52:13–53" for 52:13–53:12 and the numbering can carry an end into the next chapter (KJV Numbers 29:12–40 is Synodal 29:12–39 and 30:1): the builder cuts at the chapter's last verse (`lastVerse`), and so does the reader (`passageEnd`) — never back to the first verse. An anchor that is two KJV verses gives its links to both. The set-on-disk test checks all 735 451 links.
