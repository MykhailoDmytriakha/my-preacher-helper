# Keep and show the trail of places in the Bible

The reader keeps where the person has been — one link a place, the place being read lit — and shows it as a row that a button beside the way back shows and hides (the owner, 2026-10-20, case `2026-10-12-block-diagram`, 2.12; canvas https://claude.ai/artifact/36kE3nv1JyY1tw4ZibMA2s, row 2.12).

## How

- The rule is `frontend/app/utils/placeTrail.ts`, pure. A place is a VERSE, compared across Bibles (`sameVerse`, through `utils/versification`) — since 2026-10-27 (the owner, case `2026-10-12-block-diagram`, L11: «много таких точек»). `arriveAt(trail, place, limits)` — a verse among the last `recent` links only moves the light (`at`) there; any other verse, new or seen longer ago, joins the end; over `history` the oldest leave, never the light's link. `leaveAt` — the verse the person stopped at, on leaving: it joins the end unless it is the light's or a recent link, and the light stays. `actedVerses` — an act on several verses leaves one verse a run (its first), in the order the runs were chosen (9:10, then 7:10–12, then 8:13 → 9:10, 7:10, 8:13). READING WRITES NOTHING: a scroll neither adds a link nor moves one (it used to drag the last link along — the owner: «прокрутка вверх-вниз не должна менять истории»).
- The device keeps it: `frontend/app/components/bible/placeTrailStore.ts` — two device choices (`utils/deviceChoice.ts`): `bible:place-trail` (links, `at`) and `bible:place-trail-prefs` (`shown`, `verseStart`, `history` 10/30/50/100, `recent` 5/7/10). Nothing goes to the account: the owner set the numbers in the settings, but a move through the Bible must never be a database write. A device that refuses storage keeps the trail for the visit.
- The reader calls `arriveOnTrail` on every move to a place — the book opened at its place (any entry: a link from another book, a book chosen, Back/Forward, a reload — those land on a recent link and only light it), and within the book `arrive()` in `BibleBookReader`: a link followed, the way back, a chapter chosen, a link of the trail — and on every ACT on a verse (`actOn`): its menu opened (`verseCopy/`), a copy or a send, its links window, its badges or the sign of marks elsewhere opened (`useActedOpen` wraps the ribbon's `onOpen`), a mark made or wiped (`useReaderMarks` `onMarked`: the first verse the stroke really changed, `firstChangedVerse` in `utils/bibleMarks.ts` — an eraser drawn over words that bore nothing is no act; the owner, 2026-10-27). Leaving — `leave()`: a move away, another book, a link into another book, the way back, another screen or the page hidden — calls `leaveOnTrail` with the verse at the reading top, unless the place this reader last put on the trail is still on the screen (then the person is there). A change of translation is no leaving (the reader says it, `changeTranslationOnTrail`, and the book's next opening takes it back, `openedForTranslation` — never guessed from a place's stored Bible). A verse chosen on the minimap — pressed, its place said under the pointer — is a move (`jumpFromMap` → `arrive`); a drag along the map is reading (the owner, 2026-10-27).
- On screen: `useReaderTrail` in `PlaceTrail.tsx` gives the reader its pieces — from 1024 px the button in the book's line and a column left of the text (`beside`, `PlaceTrailColumn`); from 768 px the button in the line and the row under it; on a phone (below 768 px) the button in the app's header opens a left-edge Headless UI Dialog with the same `PlaceTrailColumn`, newest on top and the verse-start switch at its foot. The drawer is local UI state; the persisted `shown` choice still controls the wide row/column. The reader publishes its controls through `navigation/shellHeader.tsx` (`ShellHeaderProvider`, mounted beside `ShellTitlesProvider`); from 768 px the shell and reader keep their wide layouts. A trail of one link shows nothing.
- A phone drawer place closes the drawer before opening through the reader's own `openPlace`: `useFollowAfterClose.ts` is the existing reader hook, shared with its verse windows. Opening in the next frame lets the dialog release its scroll lock first, so an iOS restoration cannot undo the jump. Escape and backdrop close the drawer and Headless UI restores the trigger's focus. The phone no longer renders a bottom bar or floating trail row; the shell header collapses while scrolling down, and its height change keeps the words at their screen position. Tests: `placeTrailInReader.test.tsx` (drawer navigation, focus and dismissal) and `navigation/DashboardNav.readerHeader.test.tsx` (publication, menu feedback, collapse and the 768 px boundary).
- The column (the owner, 2026-10-26, canvas v67, row «История слева»): newest on top, so the place just reached is the first line, "←" goes to the one under it and moves the light down the column (as it moves left along the row); the light going to another link, or the links shifting, brings it into view; reading on, which only moves the lit link's verse, does not (`litLinkKey` in `utils/placeTrail.ts`, used by the row too — the column used to jump back to the top while the text was read). At its foot the switch "Verse start" (`verseStart`, off by default) puts the first words of each place's verse under it, the books' texts fetched only while it is on (`useBookTexts`, shared with the cross references, kept per Bible: the list of books stays on screen across a change of translation). The list of books (`/bible`) shows the same column from 1024 px, left of the Testaments and as tall as they are (`useTrailOnList`), the row below that. The reader widens left by the column (`readerWidth`: 1280 → 1464 px, 1544 with the verse start), so the empty margin goes first: on a 1680 px screen the text keeps its 1110 px; on 1440 it gives up about 88 px; an iPad standing (820) keeps the row. The reader only places `beside` and `readerWidth`; the widths live in `PlaceTrail.tsx`. Checked by the column tests in `__tests__/components/bible/placeTrailInReader.test.tsx` and live by `.cases/2026-10-12-block-diagram/demo-trail-left/live.cjs`. Every link looks the same: which are recent is the rule's business (the owner: a grey "history" part made one ask whether it can be tapped).
- The list of books (`/bible`, `BibleBookList`) shows the trail in place of "Continue" (the owner, 2026-10-20): `PlaceTrailOnList` — the trail's sign and the row (`PlaceTrailStrip` look `page`, a cut link fades at the edge), a bar as wide as the search line above it and the Testaments below, the lit link where the person stopped. A link opens the reader at its place in the Bible being read (`trailPlaceIn`), and the reader's arrival lights it or adds it — the same rule as a tap in the reader, nothing of its own. Even a trail of one link shows there (it is the "Continue"); "Continue" stays only for a device with no trail yet. Checked by `__tests__/components/bible/bibleBookList.test.tsx`.
- A link is LIT only while its verse is on the screen of the book being read (the owner, 2026-10-27: «выделяется, когда ты прямо на этом стихе»): the reader reports the verses on its screen (`seen`, from its place read on every scroll), `useReaderTrail` lights the light's link only among them (`onScreen`), and the list of books lights none. The light's link keeps `data-trail-at` all the same: the row and the column bring it into view by it.
- The way back ("←") goes left along the trail, one link at a time (the owner, 2026-10-20: the old swap between the last two places never reached a third): `linkBefore(trail, ownTrailPlace())` names it, `goBack` in `BibleBookReader` steps the light first (`stepOnTrail`) and then opens the place (`openPlace`) — the arrival lands on the lit link (`arriveAt` keeps the place on the link the light is on), so nothing is added even for an old link. Counted from the page's own place, not from the place a scroll reported: a jump that did not scroll tells the reader nothing. On the first link there is no way back, unless the Bible was opened from a screen of the app ("← to the note"). A cross reference within the same chapter is no new place: its "← <verse>" comes first, until taken or until any other move (`returnTo`, cleared by `arrive`).
- The numbers live in Settings → Bible (`/settings/bible`, `PlaceTrailSettings.tsx`), not in the reader's "T", which is about the text (the owner, 2026-10-20). The page says it in plain words (checked by a partner reading as a layperson): a chain of chapters you moved to with a verse remembered in each; "←" back along it, the exact destination written beside the arrow (it can be the origin verse after a link in the same chapter, or the note the Bible was opened from); reading on does not grow it. A "place" in the texts is a chapter — say so where the rule depends on it (the recent rule). The sample is `PlaceTrailSample`, drawn with the strip's and the button's own classes, pressing nothing.

## Traps

- The trail's labels are the reader's place: when that is wrong, the trail is wrong. On an iPad-width screen a link into another book was let go one verse early — the browser's scroll anchoring moved the pane as the chips arrived, and the reader took it for the person's scroll; the trail read "Исх 12:11" after "Open Исх 12:12" (BUG-20261020-ipad-arrival-place-verse-early). While a verse jumped to holds, the reading surface has anchoring off (`readingSurface.anchoring`).
- A new move to a place must call `arrive()` before `jumpTo`; calling `jumpTo` alone lets `readOn` drag the current link to the new place instead of adding or lighting one — the reader test "the way back to the verse only lights its link" catches it.
- Diagnostics keep letters and hyphens only: a number from the settings is written as a word (`placeTrailStore.ts`).
